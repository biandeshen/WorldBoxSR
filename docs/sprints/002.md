# Sprint 002 — Settlements occupy space

## Goal

Turn settlements from local population centers into spatial social entities while preserving determinism, headless scalability, and historical rise/fall semantics.

## Why now

Sprint 001 proved that geography, humans, settlements, history, inspection, long-run experiments, and 10k-scale simulation all work together. Settlement cohesion and abandonment also showed that small local social rules can create meaningful history without global fertility tuning.

The next missing contract is **space**: a settlement has a center and members, but no land it can call its own. Kingdoms, conquest, borders, resource accounting, migration, and many useful God-game interactions all depend on a stable territorial substrate.

## Execution order

1. **Territory cells v0** — deterministic ownership of nearby passable land by active settlements; no war/economy yet.
2. **Client territory observation** — inspect ownership and boundaries without moving authority into the client.
3. **Post-social 100×200 baseline** — compare population, settlement counts/lifetimes, abandoned sites, territory coverage, and runtime.
4. **History timeline v0** — make founded/abandoned and player-caused events inspectable with their causal references.
5. **Resource-accounting decision** — use evidence from territory/population behavior to decide whether settlement storage/production is warranted next.

## Non-goals

- kingdoms or rulers;
- diplomacy/war/conquest;
- buildings/jobs/economy;
- religion/culture/technology;
- elaborate art;
- large engine rewrites without benchmark evidence.

## Quality gates

Every authoritative mechanic must preserve deterministic replay and save/load. Optional mechanics must not perturb unrelated sequential RNG merely by consuming extra random draws. Behavioral changes need multi-seed evidence, and performance-sensitive changes should use the benchmark instead of intuition.
