# Sprint 004 — Measure settlement scarcity before buffering it

Status: **completed — scarcity measured; storage v1 not justified** (#81 / #82)

## Goal

Measure **settlement-level scarcity episodes** in the unmodified baseline simulation before designing any storage v1 or other growth mechanic.

The sprint is derived-only. It changes no world behavior.

## Observation model

At a 30-day research cadence, every active settlement exposes continuous territorial resource state plus three separate conditions:

1. **one-meal territorial shortage** — owned food is below `population * foodPerMeal`;
2. **local meal-path blockage** — at least one hungry member has less than the existing edible minimum `0.2` on its current tile and every one-step passable neighbor;
3. **access mismatch** — local blockage exists while the territory still holds at least one meal per current member.

A bounded external tracker records consecutive sampled episodes without entering authoritative world state or retaining raw daily trajectories.

## Validation

Permanent artificial-world tests cover:

- territorial shortage vs access mismatch;
- exact existing `0.2` meal-path semantics;
- non-hungry exclusion;
- episode start/end and later abandonment;
- bounded retention with exact aggregate counts;
- exact snapshot/RNG neutrality;
- cadence/cap/time validation.

The pre-probe branch passed **131/131 tests + smoke**.

## Six-seed 200-year baseline

Seeds: `1/4/9/45/80/98`  
World: `24×24`, 30 founders  
Cadence: 30 days

Six-seed median settlement-sample shares:

- one-meal territorial shortage: **40.74%**;
- local meal-path blockage: **29.00%**;
- access mismatch: **0.00%**;
- access mismatch as a share of local blockage: **0.00%**.

Per-seed access mismatch was zero in 1/4/45/80/98. Only seed9 showed a tail: **2.52%** of settlement-samples, or **8.19%** of its local-blockage samples.

### Interpretation

Real aggregate territorial scarcity is common. Local blockage is **not generally** a pathing artifact over abundant settlement territory.

However scarcity is also not a settlement-survival classifier. In seeds 1/4/9/80, every year100 settlement was non-declining by year200 despite substantial second-century shortage burden. Dense settlements can grow while living under persistent resource pressure.

## Seed98 Pineford sentinel

Pineford (#5) is decisive:

- year100: pop11, food remaining ~96.06%, meal coverage **21.07×** per member;
- year120: pop8, ~97.81%, **29.50×**;
- year140: pop7, ~98.18%, **33.85×**;
- year160: pop3, ~99.37%, **79.93×**;
- abandoned before year180.

At all sampled checkpoints it had:

- 0 hungry members;
- 0 blocked hungry members;
- no territorial shortage;
- no local blockage;
- no access mismatch.

Across the full tracker history Pineford had **zero episodes of all three types**.

Its 11 → 8 → 7 → 3 → 0 collapse is therefore not a food scarcity or meal-access failure under the current model.

## Decision

### Keep the research instrument

The scarcity snapshot and bounded episode tracker are useful permanent analysis infrastructure. They provide a clean language for future resource experiments and remain behavior-neutral.

### Do not build storage v1 now

The storage gate does not pass:

- true scarcity exists, but it is compatible with strong growth;
- access mismatch is usually rare;
- the only abandonment in the sampled cohort has zero scarcity signal;
- a generic buffer would mainly shift carrying-capacity dynamics rather than target the unexplained failure class.

Do not parameter-search a new store.
