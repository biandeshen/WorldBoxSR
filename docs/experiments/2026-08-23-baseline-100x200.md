# Baseline: 100 seeds × 200 years

Date: 2026-08-23  
Model: post-#2 land/water passability  
Purpose: establish a reproducible behavioral and performance baseline before settlement formation.

## Parameters

- seeds: 1–100
- years per seed: 200
- map: 24 × 24
- founders: 30 humans
- water level: 0.28
- Node.js: v22.16.0
- host: x86_64, 5 logical CPUs available to the container
- no balance parameters were changed during this experiment

## Reproduce model outcomes

The repository's deterministic batch runner reproduces the world outcomes:

```bash
npm run lab -- --start-seed 1 --seeds 100 --years 200 --width 24 --height 24 --population 30 --json
```

For the measurement run, seeds were isolated into separate Node processes and executed in bounded parallel groups. This does not change simulation results; it prevents one process/GC episode from blocking the rest of the experiment. Each seed had a 20-second process deadline during the parallel run. All 100 seeds completed.

Extinction rate at year 200: **0 / 100 (0%)**.

\* Runtime numbers came from the isolated parallel measurement run and therefore include CPU contention. They are useful as a regression baseline for this environment, not as a universal benchmark.

The seven completed measurement groups used roughly **175 seconds of wall time** in total when run sequentially as bounded parallel groups. Aggregate per-process elapsed time was about **1,207 seconds** across all seeds.

## Strong signals

### 1. Geography strongly controls carrying population

Land fraction and year-200 population have a Pearson correlation of **0.872**. This is expected and desirable at this stage: water removes both traversable space and food capacity.

High-population examples:

| Seed | Population | Land fraction |
| --- | ---: | ---: |
| 80 | 856 | 74.65% |
| 21 | 850 | 76.22% |
| 4 | 825 | 70.31% |
| 62 | 819 | 73.61% |
| 86 | 791 | 68.40% |

Low-land worlds mostly settle at much smaller populations, e.g. seed 57 ends at 217 population with 20.31% land.

### 2. Most mature worlds are resource-constrained

The median world has only **1.96%** of total food capacity sitting unused at year 200. The simulation therefore tends to grow until it continuously consumes nearly all renewable food.

That is a useful carrying-capacity feedback loop, but it also means future mechanics that consume food (settlements, armies, disasters) can easily destabilize the current equilibrium.

### 3. Seed 45 is a demographic-collapse outlier

Seed **45** ends with only **8 humans** despite having **65.28% land** and a single connected landmass of 376 tiles. Food is abundant: **99.86% remains unused** at year 200.

A focused replay shows:

- founders: 30 total, 26 female / 4 male;
- year 40: 46 population;
- year 100: 23 population;
- year 160: 16 population;
- year 200: 8 population;
- total births over 200 years: only 41.

This is not a resource collapse and not island fragmentation. The likely mechanism is a **low-density demographic / encounter failure**: humans diffuse across a large landmass while reproduction requires an eligible male within the local 3×3 neighborhood. Once population density falls, mating encounters become rare and the decline reinforces itself.

Seed 45 should be preserved as a regression/design seed before changing movement, settlement clustering, or mating behavior.

### 4. Runtime has a long but manageable tail

The slowest measured seeds were 5 (20.36 s), 27 (19.64 s), and 52 (18.61 s) under the parallel measurement setup. Runtime correlates only weakly with land fraction (0.236), so population/land area alone do not explain all cost.

The current daily simulation is still acceptable for the v0 prototype, but a 100×200 batch is already expensive enough that Simulation Lab should eventually gain first-class worker isolation/parallelism rather than relying on one long in-process loop.

## Decisions / follow-ups

1. **Do not tune birth rate yet.** Seed 45 is a structural encounter problem, not evidence that global fertility is too low.
2. Keep seed 45 as a regression seed for settlement clustering / movement changes.
3. Continue to settlement v0. Settlements may naturally solve part of the low-density encounter problem by creating persistent population centers; measure before adding a special mating fix.
4. Track Simulation Lab parallelism as tooling work, but do not block gameplay progress on it.
5. Repeat this exact 100×200 baseline after settlement formation lands so we can compare distributions rather than judging single worlds by eye.
