# Post-social baseline — 100 seeds × 200 years

Date: 2026-08-23  
Issue: #28  
World: 24×24, 30 founders, seeds 1–100, 200 simulated years

## Purpose

Re-establish the empirical baseline after Settlement v0, minimal settlement cohesion, settlement abandonment lifecycle, and behavior-neutral territory are all active. This is a multi-change comparison with the earlier pre-social baseline, **not** a controlled single-mechanic A/B test.

## Reproduce

The Simulation Lab now supports resumable per-seed NDJSON checkpoints for long experiments:

```bash
npm run lab -- \
  --start-seed 1 \
  --seeds 100 \
  --years 200 \
  --workers 1 \
  --timeout-ms 30000 \
  --checkpoint /tmp/worldboxsr-post-social.ndjson \
  --resume \
  --json
```

The hosted execution environment throttled long-lived CPU-heavy parent processes despite reporting five logical CPUs. The final dataset was therefore collected with the **same simulation parameters** in short isolated/resumable shards. Every completed seed was synchronously checkpointed. Final integrity check: **100 unique seeds, 100 successes, 0 failures, 0 duplicates, no missing seeds**.

Per-seed isolated elapsed time had median 6.75 s (range 1.26–12.17 s), but these timings span changing host CPU quota conditions and should not be compared directly with the earlier parallel-runtime baseline. The authoritative benchmark remains the dedicated 1k/10k benchmark from #21.

## Headline result: social structure fixed the bad tail without moving the whole distribution

| Year-200 metric | Pre-social baseline | Post-social baseline | Change |
| --- | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| extinction rate | 0% | 0% | — |
| population min | 8 | **128** | +120 |
| population P25 | 376 | 380.25 | +4.25 |
| population median | 483 | 489 | +6 |
| population mean | 495.7 | 496.68 | +0.98 |
| population P75 | 608 | 604.5 | -3.5 |
| population max | 856 | 871 | +15 |
| births median | 2,968 | 2,950 | -18 |
| deaths median | 2,488.5 | 2,497 | +8.5 |
| food remaining median | 1.965% | 2.004% | +0.040 pp |

The center of the demographic distribution is essentially unchanged. The large change is the floor: the old seed-45 low-density failure moved from 8 survivors to 128 after settlement cohesion. This is exactly the intended shape of the social mechanic: reduce catastrophic over-diffusion without applying a global fertility multiplier.

Seed 45 still matches its deliberate post-cohesion regression exactly at year 200:

- population: 128
- births / deaths: 184 / 86
- settlements: 7 active, 0 abandoned
- settled population: 104
- territory coverage: 71.81%
- food remaining: 96.45%

By year 200, a typical world therefore has about seven active settlements, roughly 86% of living humans attached to a settlement, and roughly 83% of passable land claimed. Radius 3 still leaves meaningful wilderness across all 100 worlds.

### Abandonment is currently rare under cohesion

Only **3 of 100 worlds** contain any abandoned settlement at year 200, and each has exactly one:

- seed 49: 1 abandoned of 8 historical settlements
- seed 62: 1 abandoned of 13
- seed 98: 1 abandoned of 7

This does not mean the lifecycle is unnecessary — it is already covered by natural declining-world and synthetic lifecycle regressions — but it shows that current cohesion + abundant local settlement membership usually keeps established settlements alive through 200 years.

## Structural outliers

- **Lowest population:** seed 45 — 128; this is the intentional demographic sentinel and still has 96.45% food remaining.
- **Highest population:** seed 80 — 871; seed 80 was also the largest-population seed in the old baseline (856), so the high-population geography remains structurally stable.
- **Most historical settlements:** seed 62 — 13 total, 12 active, 1 abandoned; 744 settled humans.
- **Lowest territory coverage:** seed 67 — 66.54%; it also has the lowest settled-population share, 69.78%.
- **Highest territory coverage:** seed 16 — 93.64%.
- **Lowest food remaining:** seed 82 — 1.47%.
- **Largest abandonment share:** seed 98 — 1 of 7 historical settlements (14.29%).

## Correlations and what they mean

- land fraction vs population: **0.906** (old baseline: 0.872)
- land fraction vs active settlements: **0.822**
- active settlements vs population: **0.800**
- territory coverage vs population: **0.035**
- settled-population share vs population: **-0.140**

The strongest driver is still geography/carrying capacity, not territory coverage. That is expected: territory v0 is deliberately behavior-neutral. A world can claim more land without receiving a hidden population bonus.

The strong active-settlement/population relationship should also not be read as settlements causing population by itself; both are strongly related to available land and population density. The current experiment is descriptive, not causal isolation of that relationship.

## Decision: do not start kingdoms or war yet

The post-social system is stable enough to continue: no extinctions, no global population inflation, no territory-driven demographic drift, and no performance pathology attributable to territory.

The next simulation step should be **settlement resource accounting before settlement resource behavior**:

1. attribute current food, food capacity, and consumption to each active settlement using its authoritative territory and current membership;
2. expose per-settlement surplus/deficit and per-capita resource pressure in metrics/inspector;
3. run multi-seed observations to determine whether territorial resource differences actually predict settlement growth, decline, or abandonment;
4. only then decide whether shared storage/production should become behavioral.

Why this comes before kingdoms/war: territory currently has ownership but no consequence. Resource accounting is the smallest evidence-producing bridge from “this land belongs to a settlement” to future economy, migration, conflict, and conquest without inventing those systems prematurely.
